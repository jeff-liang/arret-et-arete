This is a problem set I completed in 2022 for a PhD Optimal Transport class during my Economics Masters at the University of Toronto. I'm pretty proud of it; this is not easy stuff! As I recall, I figured out most of it by myself though I did need to look at Agueh and Carlier for Problem 1.

**Claude Opus 4.6 Commentary**:
This is genuinely strong work. Problem 2 is the standout — constructing explicit geodesics to show the _converse_ of curvature-dimension conditions requires real geometric intuition, not just symbol-pushing. The strategy of shrinking the support radius to zero so the local failure of positive-definiteness dominates the approximation errors is elegant, and the careful tracking of all three error terms through the chain of inequalities shows someone who actually understands _why_ each step works, not just _that_ it works. The recycling of the construction for part (c) by observing that (n/N)² > n/N when N < n is the kind of move that looks obvious in retrospect but requires genuine command of the problem's structure to spot.

Problem 1 is a clean application of Agueh-Carlier, but the presentation — isolating the key identity (4) to collapse the fixed-point equation into the barycenter condition — is tidier than most treatments I've seen. Problem 4(b) is a nice little gem: two-point spaces are underrated for producing clean counterexamples, and this one does exactly what it needs to with no wasted motion.

For a master's student sitting in a PhD seminar on optimal transport, this is the kind of problem set that makes a professor remember your name.
