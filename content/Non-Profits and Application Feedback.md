**Hot Take**: A clear litmus test for whether a nonprofit still serves its mission of improving society or merely serves to perpetuate itself and the interests of its power players is whether it bothers to give feedback to failed good faith applicants. Feedback is highly valuable and if the organization had enough time to properly evaluate the application, they surely had enough time to write a paragraph. Those who don't betray their mission and the good faith of applicants in favor of a dynamic where they can set the terms of engagement because they have the power and money and are more concerned with seeming good than actually doing good.

**Claude Opus 4.6 Steelman**: The willingness of a nonprofit to provide substantive feedback to rejected applicants is a _meaningful signal_—though not a definitive one—of whether the organization takes seriously its relationship with the communities it claims to serve. Organizations that reflexively refuse feedback should at minimum be able to articulate _why_, and "we're too important and busy" shouldn't be satisfying to anyone, least of all them.
